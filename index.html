About The Arcade

Why this exists, what it is testing, and how the prototypes are being built.

Skab Digital Reality Arcade started as a practical experiment: could small browser games become a low-cost way to learn game design, test ideas quickly, and turn creative momentum into playable proof?

I am not building this as a studio launch or storefront. I am building it as a public workshop: a place where rough ideas can become playable loops, where broken designs can be redesigned, and where the process is allowed to stay visible instead of hidden behind a finished-product mask.

The arcade also reflects how I tend to think: systems, signals, routes, recovery, pressure, feedback, and validation. Most of these games are small, but the method behind them is serious: define the loop, build the simplest playable version, test what feels wrong, patch one thing at a time, and keep the work moving.

Purpose

The goal is not to pretend every prototype is complete. The goal is to create enough playable evidence to learn what deserves to grow, what needs to be redesigned, and what should be retired.

Design LockCapture the core idea, player verb, theme, controls, and what should not be changed during a patch.
Playable LoopBuild the smallest version that can be played, judged, and improved without needing a full production cycle.
Field TestRun the prototype on real devices, especially phones, and look for friction, confusion, dead space, and broken links.
Patch DisciplineImprove one focused area at a time: controls, visuals, difficulty, layout, feedback, or deployment stability.
> METHOD: IDEA → LOOP → TEST → PATCH → PROOF
> RULE: SMALL ENOUGH TO FINISH. REAL ENOUGH TO JUDGE.
