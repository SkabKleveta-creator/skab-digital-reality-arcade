Slide 1

What this is

Plain language

Connection Trace is the Connectivity Layer: a systems-thinking mini-game and diagnostic puzzle about relationships. The player does not connect everything. The player identifies the critical bad connection.

Why it exists

Systems rarely fail everywhere at once. They fail at one relationship that everyone trusts and no one verifies. This game makes that failure visible, findable, and fixable.

Slide 2

Core concept

Eli enters the system's internal network layer. The Connection Scanner reveals hidden relationships between operators, terminals, dashboards, freight states, manual workarounds, relay nodes, verification points, and output gates.

The player must identify the critical bad connection, stabilize it, and validate the restored path. Success is not activity. Success is a verified route.

Slide 3

What it is / what it is not

Connection Trace is

  • A systems-thinking mini-game
  • A connectivity-mapping challenge
  • A diagnostic puzzle about relationships
  • A game about identifying the connection that matters

Connection Trace is not

  • A generic wiring puzzle
  • A connect-everything puzzle
  • A dashboard simulator
  • A quiz
  • A combat game
  • A clone of an existing puzzle game

Slide 4

Core loop

Observe: Read the visible system state and the conflicting claims.
Scan: Reveal hidden relationships between nodes.
Reveal: See link conditions: stable, unverified, broken, stale, duplicate, manual, false green.
Mark: Identify the candidate critical bad connection.
Stabilize: Repair the correct link, not every link.
Validate: Send a test packet and prove the route carries verified truth.

Slide 7

Win condition

1. Eli scans enough of the system.
2. The player identifies the critical bad connection.
3. Eli stabilizes the correct link.
4. A test packet reaches the correct verified outcome.
Success language: "Test packet reached verified route status. Connection stabilized."
Learning language, not punishment: "Non-critical link stabilized. Route confidence remains unresolved. System improved, but the critical connection is still unstable."

Slide 8

Screen format

Connection Trace should feel like walking through a living systems diagram.

Play space

Top-down systems room. Eli moves around nodes; the scanner reveals hidden links.

Detail panel

Right-side panel shows node and link details as they are scanned.

HUD + validation

Bottom HUD shows objective and scanner energy. A validation strip shows the test packet result.

Slide 9

Current prototype: The Dock Disagreement

Scenario

The shipping board says Truck 12 departed an hour ago. The loading dock says it never moved. Both cannot be true. The board is wired to the plan, not the dock. One wire is crossed. Find it, fix it, and prove which one is real.

Controls

WASD / arrows = walk
Space = scan
Shift = trace
J = repair
V = validate

Play Dock Disagreement Prototype

Best experienced in landscape orientation. The prototype proves the scan → trace → repair → validate loop against one crossed wire and a live truth check.

Slide 10

Guardrails

Do not make it connect-everything. The skill is finding the connection that matters.
Do not make it combat. The pressure is diagnostic, not violent.
Do not turn it into a generic wiring puzzle. Relationships carry meaning, not just current.
Do not let a green dashboard equal truth. Validation is the win condition.
Do not remove validation. A repair without a test packet is a claim, not a fix.
Do not punish wrong repairs harshly. Use learning language and keep the run alive.
