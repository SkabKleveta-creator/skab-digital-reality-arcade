Initial Question

Can diagnosis itself become gameplay without turning the player into a generic fixer who repairs everything on the board?

Root Cause

Earlier systems and mini-games risked becoming action loops when the stronger concept was diagnostic judgment. Connection Trace emerged from the need to separate observation, scan, mark, stabilize, and validate into a clearer reasoning loop.

Ken’s Logic

  • Do not make the player fix everything.
  • Make the player identify the critical bad connection.
  • Separate scanning from judging so the loop is about interpretation, not reflex alone.
  • Use the game to explore decision stewardship at small scale.

Observed Failure Modes

  • The concept could become too abstract for quick play.
  • Too many steps could slow the arcade feel.
  • The puzzle risked feeling like work unless the feedback loop was satisfying.
  • The system needed clearer boundaries between observation, scan, reveal, mark, stabilize, and validate.

Corrections Applied

  • Moved the game into design review instead of forcing a weak playable loop.
  • Clarified the current design direction around Observe, Scan, Reveal, Mark, Stabilize, Validate.
  • Locked the core rule that the player identifies the critical bad connection.
  • Deferred publication until the diagnostic loop is readable.

The Proof Is

The proof is not the game by itself. The proof is the visible trail from intent, build decision, failure mode, correction, and reusable finding.

  • Connection Trace is documented in the arcade README as a redesign item.
  • The design direction captures the diagnostic sequence explicitly.
  • The project was not forced into public release simply to fill a card slot.
  • The decision to hold and redesign is evidence of governance over momentum.

Finding

A project can become more credible by being held back. The strongest evidence from Connection Trace is not a playable page yet; it is the choice to preserve the core diagnostic idea instead of publishing a diluted loop.

Relevance Beyond the Game

Maps directly to organizational problem-solving: leaders do not need to fix every symptom; they need to identify the critical broken relationship that prevents the system from validating truth.
