Initial Question

Can a governed AI-assisted workflow maintain design continuity across multiple sectors, objectives, enemies, controls, and player-facing language without drifting away from the original intent?

Root Cause

Earlier AI-assisted builds showed that playable progress could be created quickly, but repeated patches also introduced drift, vocabulary instability, inconsistent mechanics, and regression risk. FPS-002 became the stress test for whether stricter operating rules could keep improvement from becoming uncontrolled redesign.

Ken’s Logic

  • Treat the current index.html as the source of truth, not memory or prior preference.
  • Apply one focused patch at a time so failures can be isolated.
  • Use acceptance criteria, locked vocabulary, and stop conditions to prevent accidental redesign.
  • Test whether stability improves more from governance than from stronger prompts alone.

Observed Failure Modes

  • AI drift across repeated patches.
  • Feature improvement creating new bugs.
  • Vocabulary inconsistency around signal, gates, drones, and objectives.
  • Risk of proving the concept instead of improving it under pressure.

Corrections Applied

  • One-patch workflow with stop-after-report discipline.
  • Locked terminology for Signal Gate, Automaton, Replicator Station, Signal Baton, signal-cut, scan, interrupt, stabilize, validate, and collapse command.
  • Regression review after each patch.
  • Acceptance checkpoints focused on whether the live player experience improved without breaking core loop.

The Proof Is

The proof is not the game by itself. The proof is the visible trail from intent, build decision, failure mode, correction, and reusable finding.

  • Playable public FPS-002 link exists outside the arcade repository.
  • Operating doctrine and patch discipline were formalized during the build.
  • Multiple iterations exposed that governance, not prompting alone, controlled project stability.
  • The project produced reusable operating rules for later AI-assisted builds.

Finding

Governance produced more stability than additional prompt cleverness. The build showed that AI acceleration requires explicit source-of-truth discipline, not just better instructions.

Relevance Beyond the Game

Directly relevant to enterprise AI adoption, where teams often generate outputs faster than they can govern intent, terminology, testing, and change control.
