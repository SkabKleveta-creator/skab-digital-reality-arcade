Node Blaster

Movement and target-prioritization prototype used to test continuous flow through a digital world.

Review posture: Draft evidence page. Not final. Not approved for publication as a formal claim until Ken reviews the evidence and promotes it into the official record.

1. Exhibit

Node Blaster is presented as an evidence exhibit inside the Skab Digital Reality Arcade proof trail. The game is the visible artifact; the review record documents what the build tested, what broke, what changed, and what the work proves or does not yet prove.

2. Initial Question

Can left-to-right continuous movement better express travel through a digital system than static up/down combat framing?

3. Root Cause

The original framing did not fully match the desired feel of moving through a digital world. The build needed to test whether orientation and flow changed the identity of the game.

4. Ken’s Logic

Ken identified that the game was mechanically close but directionally wrong. Shifting to left-to-right flow turned the prototype from a local shooter into a traversal experience.

5. Observed Failure Modes

  • Initial movement orientation mismatch
  • Need for continuous-run feel
  • Risk of static play
  • Potential confusion between combat and system traversal

6. Corrections Applied

  • Left-to-right redesign
  • Continuous-run direction
  • Flow-first framing
  • Digital-travel identity

7. The Proof Is

  • HTML prototype iteration
  • Directional redesign decision
  • Continuous play request
  • Evidence review shell

8. Finding

A small design orientation change can reveal the intended product identity. The prototype was not wrong; it was pointed the wrong way.

9. Relevance Beyond the Game

This maps directly to business engineering: sometimes the solution is not more features but reorienting the operating model around the right flow.
