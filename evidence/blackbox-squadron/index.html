Blackbox Squadron

Arcade shooter prototype used to test rapid deployment, release discipline, and public artifact creation.

Review posture: Draft evidence page. Not final. Not approved for publication as a formal claim until Ken reviews the evidence and promotes it into the official record.

1. Exhibit

Blackbox Squadron is presented as an evidence exhibit inside the Skab Digital Reality Arcade proof trail. The game is the visible artifact; the review record documents what the build tested, what broke, what changed, and what the work proves or does not yet prove.

2. Initial Question

Can a browser-based arcade prototype move from rough idea to public playable artifact quickly without abandoning review discipline?

3. Root Cause

The build needed to test whether AI acceleration could produce a visible artifact fast enough to matter while still preserving enough structure to be inspectable later.

4. Ken’s Logic

Ken used the game as a speed-and-discipline test. The visible output was an arcade game; the deeper test was whether ambiguity could become a deployed artifact with traceable decisions, scope control, and lessons learned.

5. Observed Failure Modes

  • Visual quality gaps
  • Risk of mistaking output speed for product maturity
  • Early build limitations
  • Need for stronger evidence linkage
  • Potential overclaiming from a single prototype

6. Corrections Applied

  • Public demo framing
  • Evidence-page structure
  • Review log requirement
  • Approval status controls
  • Separation between artifact and proof trail

7. The Proof Is

  • Playable prototype
  • Project card and visual collateral
  • Known public deployment context
  • Documented build logic
  • Evidence archive page with pending review state

8. Finding

The limiting factor was not just coding speed. The limiting factor was whether speed could be paired with scope control, review discipline, and honest evidence.

9. Relevance Beyond the Game

Rapid prototyping has business value only when the result is inspectable, explainable, and tied to a decision path.
