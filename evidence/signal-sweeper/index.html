Signal Sweeper

Containment and cleanup arcade prototype used to test readable systems under increasing difficulty.

Review posture: Draft evidence page. Not final. Not approved for publication as a formal claim until Ken reviews the evidence and promotes it into the official record.

1. Exhibit

Signal Sweeper is presented as an evidence exhibit inside the Skab Digital Reality Arcade proof trail. The game is the visible artifact; the review record documents what the build tested, what broke, what changed, and what the work proves or does not yet prove.

2. Initial Question

Can a single-screen arcade containment game teach cleanup, signal restoration, and escape logic quickly?

3. Root Cause

The arcade needed a fast, readable prototype that could prove whether players understand system-repair mechanics without long onboarding.

4. Ken’s Logic

Ken pushed for a game that could be understood in seconds but still expose deeper lessons about space, threat, repair, and signal flow.

5. Observed Failure Modes

  • Empty-space imbalance
  • Controller placement issues
  • Too much visible map at once
  • Difficulty progression gaps
  • Need for scroll-aided discovery

6. Corrections Applied

  • Larger visual output
  • Controller adjustments
  • Scrolling/map visibility changes
  • Additional levels
  • Increasing difficulty

7. The Proof Is

  • Playable arcade game
  • Level-building requests
  • UX correction trail
  • Evidence page
  • Review log placeholder

8. Finding

Readability and progression must evolve together. Adding content without changing how players discover and control it creates weak engagement.

9. Relevance Beyond the Game

For AI-enabled systems, the lesson is direct: complexity must be staged. Users need enough visibility to act, not so much that the system loses tension.
